Why TikTok lowers your video quality (and how to keep it)

Blocky shadows, smeared fast motion, soft text: almost every editor has seen their work look worse on TikTok than in the export. This is compression, and you can reduce it.

What compression does

To stream videos to millions of people, TikTok re-encodes every upload at a lower bitrate. Detail that takes a lot of data to describe, like fast motion, flashes, particles, grain and dark gradients, is the first to go.

Edits that suffer the most

  • Fast transitions, shakes and flashes.
  • Dark scenes with smooth gradients.
  • Heavy glow, grain and particle effects.
  • Small text and thin lines.

Ways to reduce it

  1. Export clean: 1080p, H.264, a standard frame rate.
  2. Avoid adding noise or grain on top of the whole edit.
  3. Brighten very dark scenes slightly so the shadows hold.
  4. Upload from a computer instead of the phone app.
  5. Use an upload tool made for editors, like Slerbo Enhancer's Crystal HD+ and HQ Turbo modes.
